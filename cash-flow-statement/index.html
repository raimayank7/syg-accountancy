Cash flow statement (indirect method), solved

A company can show a profit and still run out of cash. The cash flow statement shows where cash actually came from and where it went.

What it is

A cash flow statement shows the inflows and outflows of cash and cash equivalents during a period, classified under three activities. It is prepared according to Accounting Standard 3 (Revised). Cash means cash in hand and demand deposits with banks. Cash equivalents are short-term, highly liquid investments that are readily convertible into known amounts of cash, such as treasury bills and short-term deposits, normally with a maturity of three months or less.

The three activities

ActivityMeaningExamples
OperatingThe main revenue-producing activities of the businessCash from customers; cash paid to suppliers and employees; income tax paid
InvestingBuying and selling long-term assets and investments not included in cash equivalentsPurchase or sale of machinery, land, investments; interest and dividend received (for a non-finance company)
FinancingChanges in owners' capital and borrowingsIssue of shares or debentures; repayment of loans; interest and dividend paid

For a finance company, interest paid and interest and dividend received are operating items, because lending and investing are its main business. Transactions that involve no cash, such as issuing shares to buy machinery, are left out of the statement.

Cash from operating activities: the indirect method

Start from net profit and remove everything in it that is not an operating cash flow.

Format
Particulars
Net profit before tax and extraordinary items
Add: Non-cash and non-operating expenses: depreciation, goodwill written off, loss on sale of assets, interest paid
Less: Non-operating incomes: profit on sale of assets, interest and dividend received
Operating profit before working capital changes
Add: Decrease in current assets; increase in current liabilities
Less: Increase in current assets; decrease in current liabilities
Cash generated from operations
Less: Income tax paid
Net cash from operating activities

Why working capital changes matter: if debtors rise by ₹15,000, that much of the year's sales has not yet turned into cash, so it is deducted. If creditors rise by ₹15,000, that much of the purchases has not yet been paid for, so it is added.

Net profit before tax

The balance sheets give only the change in surplus. Work backwards: Net profit before tax = Increase in surplus (balance of Statement of Profit and Loss) + Dividend paid + Transfer to reserves + Provision for tax made during the year.

Figures in brackets are outflows. The closing figure, ₹1,20,000, agrees with the balance sheet, which proves the statement.

What the statement tells us

Godavari Ltd. generated ₹1,47,500 from its operations. That was enough to pay for new machinery of ₹1,30,000 and most of the interest and dividend. It raised ₹1,00,000 from shareholders and used half of it to repay debentures. This is a healthy pattern: operations paying for growth, and borrowing coming down.

Treatment of special items

  • Provision for tax: the opening provision is treated as paid during the year (operating outflow) and the closing provision as made during the year (added back to find profit before tax), unless other information is given.
  • Sale of a fixed asset: the sale proceeds are an investing inflow. The profit or loss on sale is removed from operating profit.
  • Bank overdraft and cash credit: treated as short-term borrowings; changes are financing activities.
  • Current investments and marketable securities are cash equivalents if they meet the definition.
  • Goodwill or patents written off: added back like depreciation.

Uses and limits

  • Shows the ability to generate cash and to pay dividends and debts.
  • Explains why cash rose or fell although profit moved the other way.
  • Helps in short-term planning.
  • Limits: it ignores non-cash transactions and the accrual concept, so it cannot replace the Statement of Profit and Loss; and it shows the past, not the future.

Questions students ask

Is depreciation a source of cash?

No. It is added back to net profit because it reduced profit without any cash going out. It does not bring cash in.

Under which activity is interest paid shown?

For a non-finance company, interest paid on borrowings is a financing activity, and interest received is an investing activity. For a finance company both are operating activities.

How do I check a cash flow statement?

The net increase or decrease, added to the opening cash and cash equivalents, must equal the closing cash and cash equivalents in the balance sheet.
