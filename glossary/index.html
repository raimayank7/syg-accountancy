Accountancy glossary

Account
A summarised record of all transactions relating to one person, asset, liability, expense or income.
Accounting equation
Assets = Liabilities + Capital. It holds after every transaction.
Accounting period
The span of time, usually a year, for which accounts are prepared.
Accrual basis
Recording income when earned and expenses when incurred, whether or not cash has moved.
Accrued income
Income earned in the period but not yet received. An asset.
Amortisation
Writing off the cost of an intangible asset over its useful life.
Appropriation
Distribution of profit, such as transfer to reserve or payment of dividend. Made only out of profit.
Asset
A resource owned by the business that will bring future benefit.
Authorised capital
The maximum share capital a company may issue under its memorandum.
Bad debts
Amounts due from debtors that cannot be recovered. A loss.
Balance sheet
A statement of assets, liabilities and capital on a given date.
Bank overdraft
The amount by which withdrawals from a bank account exceed the balance, as allowed by the bank. A liability.
Bank reconciliation statement
A statement explaining the difference between the cash book bank balance and the bank statement balance.
Bill of exchange
A written, unconditional order by a creditor to a debtor to pay a certain sum on a certain date.
Book value
The value of an asset in the books: cost less accumulated depreciation.
Calls in arrears
Amounts called up on shares but not paid by shareholders.
Capital
The owner's investment in the business; assets minus outside liabilities.
Capital expenditure
Spending that gives benefit for more than one year, such as buying a machine.
Capital reserve
A reserve created from capital profits; not available for dividend.
Cash book
The book that records all cash and bank receipts and payments; both a journal and a ledger.
Cash discount
A reduction allowed for prompt payment. It is recorded in the books.
Cash equivalents
Short-term, highly liquid investments readily convertible into known amounts of cash.
Closing stock
The value of unsold goods at the end of the period, valued at cost or market price, whichever is lower.
Compound entry
A journal entry with more than one debit or more than one credit.
Consignment
Sending goods to an agent to sell on behalf of and at the risk of the sender.
Contingent liability
A possible liability that depends on a future event, such as a pending court case. Shown as a note, not in the balance sheet totals.
Contra entry
An entry recorded on both sides of the cash book, such as cash deposited into bank.
Cost of goods sold
Opening stock + net purchases + direct expenses − closing stock.
Credit
The right side of an account; to enter an amount on that side.
Credit note
A document sent by a seller telling the buyer that his account has been credited, usually for goods returned.
Creditor
A person to whom the business owes money.
Current assets
Assets expected to be turned into cash or used up within twelve months or the operating cycle.
Current liabilities
Obligations due within twelve months or the operating cycle.
Debenture
A written acknowledgement of a debt by a company, carrying a fixed rate of interest.
Debit
The left side of an account; to enter an amount on that side.
Debit note
A document sent by a buyer telling the seller that his account has been debited, usually for goods returned.
Debtor
A person who owes money to the business.
Depreciation
The fall in the book value of a fixed asset from use, time or obsolescence, charged as an expense each year.
Dissolution
Closing down a firm: assets are sold, liabilities paid and accounts settled.
Double entry system
The system in which every transaction is recorded with equal debits and credits.
Drawings
Cash or goods taken from the business by the owner for personal use.
Entity concept
The assumption that the business is separate from its owner.
Equity shares
Shares that carry ownership and voting rights and receive dividend after preference shares.
Expense
A cost incurred to earn revenue.
Fictitious assets
Debit balances that are not real assets, such as an accumulated loss, shown until written off.
Financing activities
In a cash flow statement, activities that change owners' capital and borrowings.
Fixed assets
Assets held for long-term use in the business, not for resale.
Forfeiture of shares
Cancellation of shares for non-payment of calls; the amount already paid is kept by the company.
Gaining ratio
The ratio in which continuing partners acquire the share of an outgoing partner.
Going concern
The assumption that the business will continue for the foreseeable future.
Goodwill
The value of a firm's reputation, which lets it earn more than a normal return.
Gross profit
Net sales minus cost of goods sold.
Imprest system
A petty cash system in which the float is restored to a fixed amount at the end of each period.
Income and Expenditure Account
The revenue account of a not-for-profit organisation, showing surplus or deficit.
Input tax credit
Under GST, credit for tax paid on purchases, set off against tax collected on sales.
Intangible assets
Assets without physical form, such as goodwill, patents and trademarks.
Investing activities
In a cash flow statement, buying and selling long-term assets and investments.
Journal
The book of original entry in which transactions are first recorded in date order.
Ledger
The principal book containing all accounts, to which journal entries are posted.
Liability
An amount the business owes to outsiders.
Liquidity
The ability to pay short-term debts as they fall due.
Matching principle
Expenses of a period are set against the revenue of the same period.
Narration
The brief explanation written below a journal entry.
Net profit
Gross profit plus other incomes minus all indirect expenses.
Nominal account
An account of an expense, loss, income or gain.
Noting charges
The fee paid to a Notary Public for recording the dishonour of a bill.
Operating activities
The main revenue-producing activities of a business.
Operating cycle
The time between buying materials and receiving cash from customers.
Outstanding expense
An expense of the period that has not yet been paid. A liability.
Partnership deed
The written agreement among partners setting out the terms of the partnership.
Personal account
An account of a person, firm, company or bank.
Petty cash
A small sum kept for minor day to day payments.
Posting
Transferring entries from the journal or subsidiary books to ledger accounts.
Preference shares
Shares with a prior right to a fixed dividend and to repayment of capital.
Prepaid expense
An expense paid in advance for a future period. An asset.
Provision
An amount set aside out of profit for a known liability or loss whose amount is uncertain.
Prudence
Providing for all likely losses but not counting profits until they are earned.
Real account
An account of an asset or property.
Realisation Account
The account prepared on dissolution to find the profit or loss on selling assets and paying liabilities.
Receipts and Payments Account
A summary of the cash book of a not-for-profit organisation.
Reserve
Profit kept back in the business to strengthen its financial position.
Revaluation Account
The account that records changes in the values of assets and liabilities on reconstitution of a firm.
Revenue
Income from the ordinary activities of the business, mainly sales.
Revenue expenditure
Spending whose benefit is used up within the year, such as rent and wages.
Sacrificing ratio
The ratio in which old partners give up part of their profit share to a new partner.
Securities premium
The amount received on issue of shares or debentures above their face value.
Solvency
The ability to meet long-term obligations.
Subsidiary books
Special journals for transactions of one kind: purchases book, sales book, returns books, cash book.
Super profit
Average profit minus normal profit on the capital employed.
Suspense Account
A temporary account holding a trial balance difference until errors are found.
Trade discount
A reduction from the list price given by a seller to a trade buyer. It is not recorded in the books.
Trading Account
The account that shows gross profit or gross loss.
Trial balance
A list of all ledger balances, debit and credit, prepared to test arithmetical accuracy.
Voucher
Documentary evidence of a transaction.
Working capital
Current assets minus current liabilities.
Written down value
The value of an asset after deducting depreciation to date.

About these definitions

Accountancy has its own vocabulary, and many of its words mean something different in everyday speech. "Credit" in daily life suggests something good; in accounts it is only the right-hand side. "Capital" sounds like an asset, but in the books of a business it is a liability to the owner. "Provision" and "reserve" are used loosely as if they were the same, yet they are opposites in an important way: one is a charge against profit and the other is a use of profit. Getting these words exactly right is half of understanding the subject, and examiners award marks for precise definitions.

The definitions here are short on purpose. They are meant for quick revision and for the moment when a word in a question stops you. For the full explanation of a term, with examples, go to the topic page: the Class 11, Class 12 and B.Com lists show every topic. When you write a definition in an examination, add one line of example after it; a definition with an example almost always earns full marks.

Words that are often confused

  • Debtor and creditor: a debtor owes the business; the business owes a creditor.
  • Trade discount and cash discount: the first reduces the list price and is not recorded; the second rewards quick payment and is recorded.
  • Capital expenditure and revenue expenditure: benefit over many years, or within the year.
  • Provision and reserve: a charge against profit, or an appropriation of profit.
  • Revaluation Account and Realisation Account: the firm continues, or the firm closes.
  • Sacrificing ratio and gaining ratio: used on admission, or on retirement and death.
  • Receipts and Payments Account and Income and Expenditure Account: a cash summary, or the year's revenue result.
