How to use this practice

Deciding debit and credit is the one skill that every chapter of accountancy depends on. A wrong side in the journal becomes a wrong ledger, a wrong trial balance and wrong final accounts. The good news is that it is a small skill and yields quickly to drill. Do one round of the quiz a day for a week and the decision becomes automatic.

  1. Read the transaction and say the two accounts aloud before you look at the choices.
  2. For each account, say its kind and whether it rose or fell.
  3. Choose, check, and read the reason even when you are right.
  4. Note the ones you get wrong. They usually fall into a few families, listed below.

The families that cause most mistakes

  • Goods and assets. Goods bought for resale go to Purchases; things bought for use (furniture, computer, machinery) go to their own asset account.
  • Credit transactions. When no cash moves, the other account is a person: the supplier or the customer. Cash appears only when money is paid or received.
  • Receipt from a debtor. It is not a sale. The sale was recorded earlier; the receipt only reduces the debtor.
  • Owner's personal items. Cash or goods taken by the owner, and personal bills paid by the business, are Drawings.
  • Goods leaving without a sale. Fire, theft, samples, charity, owner's use: credit Purchases, at cost.
  • Year-end adjustments. Outstanding and prepaid expenses and accrued income each create a new account that goes to the balance sheet.

Questions students ask

How do I decide which account to debit?

Name the two accounts in the transaction, decide the kind of each (asset, expense, liability, capital or revenue) and whether it has increased or decreased. Assets and expenses increase by debit; liabilities, capital and revenue increase by credit.

Why is Purchases credited when goods are lost or given away?

Because those goods will no longer be sold. Crediting Purchases at cost removes them from the cost of goods available for sale.
