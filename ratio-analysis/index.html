Ratio analysis: formulas and a fully worked example

A profit of one lakh is splendid for a tea stall and poor for a factory. Ratios turn raw figures into comparisons that mean something.

What a ratio is

An accounting ratio is a relationship between two figures of the financial statements, expressed as a proportion (2 : 1), a number of times (4 times), or a percentage (30%). Ratios are useful only in comparison: with the same firm's earlier years, with other firms in the industry, or with a standard.

The four groups

GroupQuestion it answers
Liquidity ratiosCan the business pay its short-term debts?
Solvency ratiosCan it meet its long-term obligations?
Activity (turnover) ratiosHow efficiently does it use its resources?
Profitability ratiosHow much does it earn on sales and on capital?

Liquidity ratios

RatioFormulaMalabar Ltd.
Current ratioCurrent assets ÷ Current liabilities5,00,000 ÷ 2,50,000 = 2 : 1
Quick (liquid, acid-test) ratioQuick assets ÷ Current liabilities, where quick assets = current assets − inventories − prepaid expenses(5,00,000 − 2,00,000 − 50,000) ÷ 2,50,000 = 1 : 1

A current ratio of about 2 : 1 and a quick ratio of about 1 : 1 are the traditional rules of thumb. Too low a ratio signals difficulty in paying bills; too high a ratio means money lying idle in stock, debtors or the bank.

Solvency ratios

RatioFormulaMalabar Ltd.
Debt to equity ratioLong-term debt ÷ Shareholders' funds3,00,000 ÷ 6,50,000 = 0.46 : 1
Total assets to debt ratioTotal assets ÷ Long-term debt12,00,000 ÷ 3,00,000 = 4 : 1
Proprietary ratioShareholders' funds ÷ Total assets6,50,000 ÷ 12,00,000 = 0.54 : 1
Interest coverage ratioProfit before interest and tax ÷ Interest on long-term debt1,80,000 ÷ 30,000 = 6 times

A lower debt to equity ratio means more safety for lenders. An interest coverage of 6 times means the profit could fall to a sixth before interest became unpayable.

Average inventory = (opening + closing) ÷ 2 = (2,20,000 + 2,00,000) ÷ 2 = ₹2,10,000. A higher turnover means stock is sold, and debtors pay, more quickly.

Profitability ratios

RatioFormulaMalabar Ltd.
Gross profit ratioGross profit ÷ Revenue from operations × 1003,60,000 ÷ 12,00,000 × 100 = 30%
Operating ratio(Cost of revenue from operations + Operating expenses) ÷ Revenue from operations × 100(8,40,000 + 1,80,000) ÷ 12,00,000 × 100 = 85%
Operating profit ratioOperating profit ÷ Revenue from operations × 1001,80,000 ÷ 12,00,000 × 100 = 15%
Net profit ratioProfit after tax ÷ Revenue from operations × 1001,12,500 ÷ 12,00,000 × 100 = 9.375%
Return on investment (return on capital employed)Profit before interest and tax ÷ Capital employed × 1001,80,000 ÷ 9,50,000 × 100 = 18.95%

Operating ratio and operating profit ratio always add up to 100%: here 85% + 15%. Return on investment is the best single test of overall efficiency, because it relates profit to all the long-term money used to earn it.

Reading the result

Malabar Ltd. is comfortably liquid (2 : 1 and 1 : 1), lightly borrowed (0.46 : 1) with interest covered six times, turns its stock four times a year and collects from customers in about a month and a half, and earns nearly 19% on the capital employed. On these figures it is a sound business. The same ratios for last year and for a competitor would tell us whether it is improving and how it compares.

How a transaction changes a ratio

A favourite examination question. Suppose the current ratio is 2 : 1 (current assets ₹5,00,000, current liabilities ₹2,50,000).

TransactionEffect on CA and CLCurrent ratio
Paid creditors ₹50,000CA 4,50,000; CL 2,00,0002.25 : 1 (improves)
Bought goods on credit ₹50,000CA 5,50,000; CL 3,00,0001.83 : 1 (falls)
Collected ₹40,000 from debtorsCA unchanged; CL unchanged2 : 1 (no change)
Sold a fixed asset for cash ₹1,00,000CA 6,00,000; CL 2,50,0002.4 : 1 (improves)
Issued shares for cash ₹1,00,000CA 6,00,000; CL 2,50,0002.4 : 1 (improves)

Rule: when the ratio is above 1, an equal decrease in both current assets and current liabilities improves it, and an equal increase lowers it.

Limitations of ratio analysis

  • Ratios are only as good as the figures behind them; window dressing of accounts distorts them.
  • They ignore price-level changes and qualitative factors such as staff quality.
  • Different firms use different accounting policies, so comparisons can mislead.
  • A single ratio means little; ratios must be read together and over time.

Questions students ask

What is the ideal current ratio?

The traditional rule of thumb is 2 : 1, and 1 : 1 for the quick ratio. The right level differs by industry.

Which items are excluded from quick assets?

Inventories and prepaid expenses, because they cannot be turned into cash at once.

What is the difference between operating ratio and operating profit ratio?

Operating ratio is operating cost as a percentage of revenue from operations; operating profit ratio is operating profit as a percentage of the same. Together they total 100%.
