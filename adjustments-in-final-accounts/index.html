Adjustments in final accounts, with a solved example

An adjustment is a fact given after the trial balance. Because it is not yet in the books, it must be entered twice, once on each side of the double entry.

Why adjustments are needed

The trial balance shows what was recorded during the year. On the last day some things are still unrecorded: salaries due but not paid, insurance paid for next year, depreciation, stock in hand, debts likely to go bad. The accrual and matching principles require that the year's profit include all expenses and incomes that belong to the year and nothing that belongs to another year. Adjustments bring the books to that point.

The golden rule

An item inside the trial balance appears once in the final accounts. An adjustment given outside the trial balance appears twice.

Solved example: Kaveri Traders with adjustments

Take the trial balance of Kaveri Traders from the final accounts page and prepare final accounts after these adjustments:

  1. Closing stock ₹1,00,000.
  2. Salaries outstanding ₹5,000.
  3. Insurance prepaid ₹1,500.
  4. Depreciate machinery and furniture at 10%.
  5. Write off further bad debts ₹2,000 and create a provision for doubtful debts at 5% on debtors.
  6. Commission earned but not yet received ₹1,000.
  7. Allow interest on capital at 5%.

Working notes

  • Depreciation: machinery 10% of ₹1,50,000 = ₹15,000; furniture 10% of ₹50,000 = ₹5,000; total ₹20,000.
  • Debtors after further bad debts: ₹90,000 − ₹2,000 = ₹88,000. Provision at 5% = ₹4,400.
  • Interest on capital: 5% of ₹2,50,000 = ₹12,500.
  • The Trading Account is not touched by adjustments 2 to 7, so gross profit remains ₹2,10,000.

Special points

  • Adjustment inside the trial balance. If "outstanding salaries" appears in the trial balance, the adjusting entry has already been passed: show it only in the balance sheet.
  • Closing stock inside the trial balance (together with "adjusted purchases") is shown only in the balance sheet.
  • Provision on debtors is always calculated after deducting further bad debts.
  • Depreciation "per annum" on an asset bought during the year is charged only for the months held.
  • Interest on loan. If a 10% loan was outstanding all year and the trial balance shows less interest paid than a full year's, the balance is outstanding interest.
  • Manager's commission may be on net profit before charging the commission (profit × rate ÷ 100) or after charging it (profit × rate ÷ (100 + rate)).

Example of commission. Net profit before commission ₹1,10,000; commission 10%. Before charging: ₹11,000. After charging: ₹1,10,000 × 10 ÷ 110 = ₹10,000, which is 10% of the remaining ₹1,00,000.

Questions students ask

Why does an adjustment appear twice in final accounts?

Because it is not yet recorded in the books. Its double entry has two sides, and both must be shown: one in the Trading or Profit and Loss Account and one in the Balance Sheet.

Where is prepaid insurance shown?

It is deducted from insurance on the debit side of the Profit and Loss Account and shown as an asset in the Balance Sheet.

How is provision for doubtful debts calculated when there are further bad debts?

First deduct the further bad debts from debtors, then apply the percentage to the remaining debtors.
