The accounting equation, with a solved example

Whatever a business owns was paid for by someone: an outsider or the owner. That one idea is the whole equation.

The equation

Assets = Liabilities + Capital

Assets are what the business owns. Liabilities are what it owes to outsiders. Capital is what it owes to the owner. Since every asset is financed either by outsiders or by the owner, the two sides must always be equal. The equation can be turned around: Capital = Assets − Liabilities, and Liabilities = Assets − Capital.

Why it always holds

Every transaction touches at least two items, and by the same amount. This is the dual aspect. There are only four patterns:

  • One asset rises and another asset falls (buying furniture for cash).
  • An asset rises and a liability rises (buying goods on credit).
  • An asset falls and a liability falls (paying a creditor).
  • An asset and capital change together (owner brings in money; profit raises capital; expenses and drawings reduce it).

In none of these does one side change alone, so the equation cannot break.

The reasoning for each transaction

  1. Cash comes in, and the business owes the same amount to Meera: cash and capital both rise by ₹5,00,000.
  2. One asset is exchanged for another: cash falls, furniture rises. The total does not change.
  3. Again an exchange: cash falls by ₹1,20,000 and stock rises by ₹1,20,000.
  4. Stock rises by ₹80,000 and so does the amount owed to the supplier.
  5. Cash rises by ₹70,000 but stock falls by only ₹50,000, its cost. The difference of ₹20,000 is profit, and profit belongs to the owner, so capital rises by ₹20,000.
  6. Rent is an expense. Cash falls by ₹10,000 and capital falls by ₹10,000, because an expense reduces the owner's profit.
  7. Cash falls by ₹30,000 and the debt to Gupta Traders falls by the same amount.
  8. Drawings reduce cash and capital by ₹5,000 each.

Final position

Assets: cash ₹3,45,000 + furniture ₹60,000 + stock ₹1,50,000 = ₹5,55,000. Liabilities and capital: creditors ₹50,000 + capital ₹5,05,000 = ₹5,55,000. The two sides agree.

Capital in more detail

Closing capital = Opening capital + Additional capital + Profit − Drawings (or − Loss). In the example, ₹5,00,000 + profit on sale ₹20,000 − rent ₹10,000 − drawings ₹5,000 = ₹5,05,000, the figure in the last row.

Common mistakes

  • Reducing stock by the selling price. Stock always leaves at cost; the extra is profit.
  • Forgetting that expenses and losses reduce capital, and incomes and gains increase it.
  • Treating a credit purchase as a fall in cash. No cash moves until the supplier is paid.
  • Recording outstanding expenses only on one side. Salary due but unpaid raises a liability and lowers capital.

Questions students ask

What is the accounting equation?

Assets = Liabilities + Capital. It says that everything a business owns is financed either by outsiders or by the owner.

How does profit affect the accounting equation?

Profit increases capital, and an equal net increase appears in assets, usually cash or debtors. A loss or an expense reduces capital.

Can the two sides of the equation ever differ?

No. Every transaction changes two items by the same amount, so the sides stay equal. If they differ in your working, an entry is wrong or missing.
