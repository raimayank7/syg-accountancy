Accounts from incomplete records (single entry system)

Many small traders keep only a cash book and a note of who owes whom. Profit can still be found, by comparing what the owner was worth at the start and at the end.

What incomplete records are

Books that do not follow the double entry system fully are called incomplete records, or loosely the single entry system. Typically the trader keeps a cash book and personal accounts of debtors and creditors, but no accounts for sales, purchases, expenses or assets.

Limitations

  • No trial balance can be prepared, so arithmetical accuracy cannot be checked.
  • True profit and financial position are hard to find; much depends on estimates.
  • Errors and fraud are harder to detect.
  • Banks and tax authorities place less trust in such accounts.

Method 1: statement of affairs (net worth method)

Profit increases the owner's capital. So if we know capital at the beginning and at the end, the increase, after allowing for money the owner put in or took out, is the profit.

Profit = Closing capital + Drawings − Additional capital − Opening capital

Capital on any date is found from a statement of affairs: a list of assets and liabilities like a balance sheet, but built from estimates and whatever records exist.

Drawings are added back because they reduced closing capital without being a loss. Additional capital is deducted because it increased closing capital without being a profit.

Adjustments under this method

If the question asks for depreciation, provision for doubtful debts or outstanding expenses, apply them to the closing statement of affairs before finding closing capital. For example, 10% depreciation on furniture would reduce closing furniture to ₹90,000, closing capital to ₹3,10,000 and profit to ₹1,30,000.

Method 2: conversion to double entry

The statement of affairs gives one figure of profit and no detail. For a proper Trading and Profit and Loss Account, the missing figures (credit sales, credit purchases, opening capital, cash balances) are worked out by preparing summary accounts.

Questions students ask

How is profit calculated under the single entry system?

Profit = Closing capital + Drawings − Additional capital − Opening capital. Opening and closing capital are found from statements of affairs.

Why are drawings added back?

Drawings reduced the closing capital although they are not a business loss. Adding them back shows what capital would have been without them.

How are credit sales found from incomplete records?

By preparing a Total Debtors Account with opening and closing debtors, cash received, discount, bad debts and returns. Credit sales are the balancing figure on the debit side.
