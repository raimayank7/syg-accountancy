Cash book: single column, double column and petty cash

The cash book is a journal and a ledger account at once. Record cash here and you need neither a journal entry nor a separate Cash A/c.

Why a cash book

Cash transactions are the most frequent in any business. Journalising each one and then posting it would double the work. So cash receipts and payments are recorded directly in a special book, the cash book. Receipts go on the debit (left) side and payments on the credit (right) side. Because it is written from vouchers it is a book of original entry; because it shows the cash balance it also serves as the Cash A/c in the ledger.

Single column cash book

It has one amount column on each side and records cash only. The balance is always a debit balance or nil: you cannot pay out more cash than you have.

Example. Prepare the cash book of Lakshmi Stores for June 2026: June 1 cash in hand ₹12,000; June 3 cash sales ₹9,000; June 7 paid wages ₹3,500; June 12 received from Dev ₹6,000; June 18 bought stationery ₹800; June 25 paid rent ₹5,000; June 29 cash sales ₹7,300.

Double column cash book (cash and bank)

Most payments today go through the bank, so the cash book is given two amount columns on each side: Cash and Bank. Deposits and receipts by cheque or transfer go in the bank column on the debit side; payments by cheque or transfer go in the bank column on the credit side.

Contra entries

When cash is deposited into the bank, or withdrawn from the bank for office use, both sides of the entry fall inside the cash book. Such an entry is recorded on both sides and marked "C" (contra) in the folio column. It needs no posting to the ledger.

  • Cash deposited into bank: debit side, bank column "To Cash A/c (C)"; credit side, cash column "By Bank A/c (C)".
  • Cash withdrawn for office use: debit side, cash column "To Bank A/c (C)"; credit side, bank column "By Cash A/c (C)".

Solved example

Petty cash book and the imprest system

Small payments such as tea, postage, auto fare and cleaning are too many for the main cash book. They are handled by a petty cashier, who is given a fixed sum at the start of a period. At the end of the period the amount spent is given back to him, so that he starts the next period with the same fixed sum again. That fixed sum is the imprest, and the method is the imprest system.

The petty cash book is analytical: each payment is entered in the total column and again in a column for its kind of expense, so that monthly totals for each expense are ready for posting.

Advantages of the imprest system: the petty cashier can never hold more than the fixed sum, his book is checked each time he asks for reimbursement, and the main cash book is kept free of small items.

The bank column of the cash book rarely agrees with the bank's own statement. The reasons, and how to reconcile them, are on the bank reconciliation statement page.

Questions students ask

Why does a cash book always show a debit balance in the cash column?

Because payments cannot be more than the cash available. The total of the receipts side is always equal to or greater than the payments side.

What is a contra entry?

An entry that affects both the cash column and the bank column of the cash book, such as depositing cash into the bank. It is recorded on both sides and marked C.

What is the imprest system?

A system in which the petty cashier starts each period with a fixed sum and is reimbursed exactly what he spent, so the float returns to the fixed sum.
