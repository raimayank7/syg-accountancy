Consignment accounts, with a solved example

Goods sent on consignment are not sold to the agent. They stay the sender's property until the agent sells them to someone else.

What consignment is

A manufacturer or wholesaler who wants to sell in a distant market may send goods to an agent there. The sender is the consignor (principal) and the agent is the consignee. The consignee sells the goods on behalf of the consignor, at the consignor's risk, and earns a commission. The goods remain the consignor's property: unsold goods, and any loss, belong to him.

Documents and terms

  • Proforma invoice: a statement sent with the goods showing quantity, description and price. It is not a sales invoice.
  • Account sales: the periodic statement sent by the consignee showing goods sold, expenses paid, commission charged and the net amount due.
  • Ordinary commission: a percentage of sales for selling the goods.
  • Del credere commission: an extra commission for which the consignee bears the risk of bad debts on credit sales.
  • Over-riding commission: an extra commission on sales above a target or above invoice price.
  • Non-recurring expenses: spent to bring the goods to the consignee's godown: freight, insurance in transit, loading, unloading, customs duty.
  • Recurring expenses: spent after the goods reach the godown: godown rent, storage insurance, advertising, salesmen's pay.

Accounts in the consignor's books

  1. Consignment Account (one for each consignment, named after the place): a nominal account showing profit or loss on the consignment.
  2. Consignee's Account: a personal account showing what the consignee owes.
  3. Goods Sent on Consignment Account: closed by transfer to the Trading (or Purchases) Account.

Valuation of unsold stock

Stock with the consignee is valued at cost plus a proportionate share of non-recurring expenses (those incurred by the consignor and by the consignee up to the godown), or at market price if that is lower. Recurring expenses are not included, because they do not add to the value of the unsold goods.

Solved example

Anil of Delhi consigned 100 ceiling fans, costing ₹2,000 each, to Bala of Jaipur. Anil paid ₹10,000 for freight and insurance. Bala paid ₹4,000 for unloading and cartage to the godown and ₹6,000 for selling expenses. Bala sold 80 fans at ₹2,800 each and is entitled to a commission of 5% on sales. He sent an account sales with a bank draft for the balance.

Working

  • Sales = 80 × ₹2,800 = ₹2,24,000. Commission = 5% of ₹2,24,000 = ₹11,200.
  • Unsold stock = 20 fans. Cost = 20 × ₹2,000 = ₹40,000.
  • Non-recurring expenses = ₹10,000 + ₹4,000 = ₹14,000 on 100 fans; share of 20 fans = ₹2,800.
  • Value of unsold stock = ₹40,000 + ₹2,800 = ₹42,800.

Loss of goods

Normal loss is unavoidable: evaporation, leakage, breaking of bulk. It is not shown separately. Its cost is spread over the remaining good units, so the value per unit of stock rises.

Abnormal loss is avoidable or accidental: theft, fire, accident in transit. It is valued like stock (cost plus proportionate non-recurring expenses up to the point of loss) and credited to the Consignment Account, so that the profit on consignment is not distorted. Any amount recovered from insurance is debited to the insurer; the rest goes to the Profit and Loss Account.

Example of normal loss. 1,000 kg of a chemical costing ₹50 a kg is consigned with freight ₹5,000. Normal loss is 50 kg. 800 kg are sold. Total cost = ₹55,000 for 950 kg of good units. Unsold stock = 150 kg, valued at ₹55,000 × 150/950 = ₹8,684 (to the nearest rupee).

Goods sent at invoice price

The consignor may invoice the goods above cost, so that the consignee does not learn the real profit. The excess over cost is called loading. At the end, loading is removed by adjusting entries on goods sent, on opening and closing stock (through a Stock Reserve Account), so that the true profit is shown. If the fans above had been invoiced at ₹2,500 each, the loading on unsold stock would be 20 × ₹500 = ₹10,000.

Questions students ask

Who owns the goods sent on consignment?

The consignor. Ownership does not pass to the consignee, who is only an agent selling on the consignor's behalf.

How is unsold stock on consignment valued?

At cost plus the proportionate non-recurring expenses incurred to bring the goods to the consignee's godown, or at market price if lower.

What is del credere commission?

An additional commission paid to the consignee, in return for which he bears any loss from bad debts on credit sales.
