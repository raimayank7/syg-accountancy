Final accounts of a sole proprietor, solved

A year of entries comes down to two questions. How much did the business earn? What does it own and owe today? The final accounts answer both.

The three statements

  1. Trading Account: shows gross profit or gross loss, the result of buying (or making) and selling goods.
  2. Profit and Loss Account: starts with gross profit, deducts all other expenses and adds other incomes, to show net profit or net loss.
  3. Balance Sheet: a statement of assets, liabilities and capital on the last day of the year. It shows the financial position.

The first two are accounts (with "To" and "By") and cover a period: "for the year ended 31 March 2026". The balance sheet is a statement on a date: "as at 31 March 2026".

Which item goes where

StatementDebit side / LiabilitiesCredit side / Assets
Trading AccountOpening stock; purchases less returns; direct expenses: wages, carriage inwards, freight, octroi, power and fuel, factory rent and lightingSales less returns; closing stock
Profit and Loss AccountIndirect expenses: salaries, office rent, insurance, printing, advertising, carriage outwards, discount allowed, bad debts, interest paid, depreciation, repairsGross profit; commission, interest, rent and discount received; other gains
Balance SheetCapital (plus net profit, less drawings); long-term loans; creditors; bills payable; outstanding expenses; bank overdraftFixed assets; investments; stock; debtors; bills receivable; prepaid expenses; cash at bank and in hand

The test for a direct expense: is it spent to buy the goods, bring them to the place of business or make them ready for sale? If yes, Trading Account. Everything spent on administration, selling and finance goes to the Profit and Loss Account. Carriage inwards is direct; carriage outwards is indirect.

Check your work

  • Every item of the trial balance is used once: either in the Trading Account, or the Profit and Loss Account, or the Balance Sheet. Tick each as you use it.
  • Closing stock, given outside the trial balance, appears twice: credit side of the Trading Account and assets side of the Balance Sheet.
  • Net profit is added to capital; drawings are deducted.
  • If the balance sheet does not agree, look first for an unticked item, then for an item on the wrong side.

Order of the balance sheet

Assets and liabilities are arranged ("marshalled") in one of two orders. In order of permanence, the most lasting items come first: fixed assets at the top, cash at the bottom; capital first, creditors last. In order of liquidity it is the reverse: cash first, goodwill last. The example uses the order of permanence.

Useful terms from the statements

  • Cost of goods sold = Opening stock + Net purchases + Direct expenses − Closing stock. Here: 80,000 + 4,05,000 + 45,000 − 1,00,000 = ₹4,30,000.
  • Gross profit = Net sales − Cost of goods sold = 6,40,000 − 4,30,000 = ₹2,10,000.
  • Operating profit = Gross profit − operating expenses, leaving out non-operating items such as interest on loans and profit on sale of assets.
  • Working capital = Current assets − Current liabilities = (1,00,000 + 90,000 + 48,000 + 12,000) − 70,000 = ₹1,80,000.

Questions students ask

What is the difference between gross profit and net profit?

Gross profit is net sales minus the cost of goods sold, found in the Trading Account. Net profit is gross profit plus other incomes minus all indirect expenses, found in the Profit and Loss Account.

Is carriage inwards a direct expense?

Yes. It is the cost of bringing purchased goods to the business, so it goes to the Trading Account. Carriage outwards, the cost of delivering goods sold, goes to the Profit and Loss Account.

Why is the balance sheet not an account?

It is a statement of balances remaining after the nominal accounts are closed. It has no debit and credit sides and uses no "To" and "By".
