Financial statements of a company (Schedule III)

A company cannot arrange its balance sheet as it likes. The law fixes the headings and their order, so that any reader can compare any two companies.

What the financial statements are

Under the Companies Act, 2013 the financial statements of a company include a balance sheet, a statement of profit and loss, a cash flow statement (for most companies), a statement of changes in equity where applicable, and the notes to accounts. Their form is laid down in Schedule III of the Act. They are prepared in vertical form: one column of figures for the current year and one for the previous year, with a note number against each line.

Property, plant and equipment and intangible assets are further divided into: property, plant and equipment; intangible assets; capital work-in-progress; and intangible assets under development.

Current and non-current

An asset is current if it is expected to be realised, sold or used within the company's normal operating cycle or within twelve months after the balance sheet date, is held mainly for trading, or is cash or a cash equivalent. A liability is current if it is due to be settled within the operating cycle or within twelve months. Everything else is non-current. The operating cycle is the time between acquiring materials and receiving cash from customers; where it cannot be identified it is taken as twelve months.

Notes to accounts

Each line of the balance sheet and the statement of profit and loss gives only a total. The details are in numbered notes. The note on share capital, for example, shows authorised, issued, subscribed and paid-up capital, with calls in arrears deducted and forfeited shares added. The note on reserves and surplus lists each reserve with its opening balance, additions, deductions and closing balance.

Objectives and limitations of financial statements

  • They give information about financial position, performance and cash flows to shareholders, lenders, employees, government and the public.
  • They are the basis for ratio analysis and for comparative and common-size statements.
  • Limits: they are based on historical cost and ignore price changes; they leave out things that cannot be measured in money; they involve estimates and personal judgement; they show the position on one date only.

Check the current Schedule. Schedule III is amended from time to time by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, and companies that follow Ind AS use Division II of the Schedule, with a different layout. This page follows Division I, the form taught at school level. The Act and Schedule are on mca.gov.in.

Questions students ask

Where is a bank overdraft shown in a company's balance sheet?

Under Current liabilities, in the sub-head Short-term borrowings.

Where is the debit balance of the Statement of Profit and Loss shown?

As a negative figure under Reserves and surplus, within Shareholders' funds.

What is an operating cycle?

The time between acquiring assets for processing and receiving cash from their sale. If it cannot be identified it is assumed to be twelve months.
