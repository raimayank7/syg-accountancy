Retirement and death of a partner

When a partner leaves, the firm must work out everything he is owed up to that day, pay him or his family fairly, and carry on.

What the outgoing partner is entitled to

  • His capital and current account balance.
  • His share of goodwill.
  • His share of reserves and accumulated profits.
  • His share of profit on revaluation of assets and liabilities.
  • His share of profit up to the date of leaving.
  • Interest on capital and salary, if due.
  • Less: drawings, interest on drawings and his share of any loss.

New ratio and gaining ratio

The remaining partners take over the outgoing partner's share. Gain of a partner = New share − Old share. If nothing is said, the remaining partners continue in their old ratio between themselves, and the gaining ratio equals that ratio.

Example. Prem, Qadir and Rani share 3 : 2 : 1. Qadir retires. Prem and Rani will share 3 : 1.

PartnerNew shareOld shareGain
Prem3/4 = 9/123/6 = 6/123/12
Rani1/4 = 3/121/6 = 2/121/12

Gaining ratio = 3 : 1.

Treatment of goodwill

The gaining partners pay the outgoing partner for his share of goodwill, through the capital accounts. Goodwill of the firm is valued at ₹1,80,000; Qadir's share is 2/6 = ₹60,000.

Adjustment of the remaining partners' capitals

The remaining partners may decide to keep their capitals in the new ratio. Add their adjusted capitals to find the total (or use a stated total), divide it in the new ratio, and compare with what each has. A shortfall is brought in; a surplus is withdrawn or moved to current account.

Death of a partner

The accounting is the same as for retirement, with two differences. First, death can occur on any date, so the deceased partner's share of profit from the last balance sheet to the date of death must be estimated. Second, the amount due is transferred to his Executors' Account and paid to his legal representatives.

Share of profit till the date of death

It is estimated either on the basis of time or on the basis of sales.

Time basis. Qadir (2/6 share) dies on 30 June 2026, three months after the last accounts. Last year's profit was ₹2,40,000. His share = ₹2,40,000 × 3/12 × 2/6 = ₹20,000.

Journal
DateParticularsDr. (₹)Cr. (₹)
Jun 30Profit and Loss Suspense A/c   Dr.20,000
To Qadir's Capital A/c20,000
(Share of profit to the date of death)

Sales basis. Last year sales were ₹12,00,000 and profit ₹2,40,000, a rate of 20%. Sales from 1 April to 30 June this year are ₹3,60,000. Estimated profit = 20% of ₹3,60,000 = ₹72,000; Qadir's share = ₹72,000 × 2/6 = ₹24,000.

The Profit and Loss Suspense Account is closed at the year end against the actual profit. If the profit sharing ratio of the remaining partners changes, the entry is made instead through their capital accounts in the gaining ratio.

Deceased partner's capital account

It is credited with his capital, share of goodwill, reserves, revaluation profit, profit to the date of death, interest on capital and salary to that date; debited with drawings, interest on drawings and share of loss. The balance goes to the Executors' Account:

Questions students ask

What is the gaining ratio?

The ratio in which the remaining partners take over the share of the retiring or deceased partner. For each remaining partner it is new share minus old share.

How is a deceased partner's share of profit calculated?

On a time basis, by applying the period up to death to last year's or the average profit, or on a sales basis, by applying last year's rate of profit to sales up to the date of death. His share is then taken at his profit sharing ratio.

What interest is a retiring partner entitled to on the amount left unpaid?

As agreed. If there is no agreement, section 37 of the Partnership Act lets him choose 6% a year on the amount due or a share of the profit earned by using his money.
