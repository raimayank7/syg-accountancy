Goodwill: nature and methods of valuation

An old, trusted shop earns more than a new one with the same stock and furniture. The value of that extra earning power is goodwill.

Meaning

Goodwill is the value of the reputation and connections of a business, which enable it to earn more profit than a similar new business could. It is an intangible asset: it cannot be seen or touched, but it is real, because a buyer will pay for it. It cannot be sold separately from the business.

Factors affecting goodwill

  • Location: a shop on a busy road draws more customers.
  • Time: an older business has more loyal customers.
  • Nature of business: steady demand, few competitors or a licence adds value.
  • Efficient management and skilled staff.
  • Quality of goods and service.
  • Market situation: a monopoly or a strong brand.
  • Special advantages: long-term contracts, patents, assured supply of materials.

Kinds of goodwill

Purchased goodwill arises when a business is bought for more than the value of its net assets. It is recorded in the books because a price was paid for it. Self-generated goodwill is built up by the business over time. Accounting Standard 26 (Intangible Assets) does not allow self-generated goodwill to be recorded as an asset, since its cost cannot be measured reliably. This is why, on changes in a firm, goodwill is adjusted through the partners' capital accounts and not kept in the books.

When goodwill is valued in a partnership

  • Change in the profit sharing ratio.
  • Admission of a partner.
  • Retirement or death of a partner.
  • Sale or amalgamation of the firm.

Method 1: average profit

Goodwill = Average profit × Number of years' purchase

Before averaging, make the profits normal: remove abnormal gains (add back abnormal losses), and deduct a fair salary for the owners if none was charged.

Example. Profits of a firm for the last four years: ₹80,000, ₹90,000, ₹1,00,000 and ₹1,10,000. Goodwill is valued at two years' purchase of the average profit.

Average profit = (80,000 + 90,000 + 1,00,000 + 1,10,000) ÷ 4 = ₹95,000. Goodwill = ₹95,000 × 2 = ₹1,90,000.

Method 2: super profit

Any business earns some return on the money invested in it. Goodwill exists only if the firm earns more than the normal return.

  1. Capital employed = Total assets (excluding goodwill and fictitious assets) − Outside liabilities.
  2. Normal profit = Capital employed × Normal rate of return ÷ 100.
  3. Super profit = Average profit − Normal profit.
  4. Goodwill = Super profit × Number of years' purchase.

Example. Average profit ₹95,000; capital employed ₹6,00,000; normal rate of return 10%. Goodwill at three years' purchase of super profit.

Normal profit = 10% of ₹6,00,000 = ₹60,000. Super profit = ₹95,000 − ₹60,000 = ₹35,000. Goodwill = ₹35,000 × 3 = ₹1,05,000.

Method 3: capitalisation

(a) Capitalisation of average profit

  1. Capitalised value of the business = Average profit × 100 ÷ Normal rate of return.
  2. Goodwill = Capitalised value − Capital employed (net assets).

With the same figures: capitalised value = ₹95,000 × 100 ÷ 10 = ₹9,50,000. Goodwill = ₹9,50,000 − ₹6,00,000 = ₹3,50,000.

(b) Capitalisation of super profit

Goodwill = Super profit × 100 ÷ Normal rate of return = ₹35,000 × 100 ÷ 10 = ₹3,50,000. Both capitalisation routes give the same answer, which is a useful check.

The answers differ because the methods and the years of purchase differ. In practice the partners agree on a method in the deed; in an examination the question states it.

Normalising profits: a short example

Profit of the year was ₹1,20,000 after a loss by fire of ₹15,000 (abnormal) and including a gain of ₹10,000 on sale of an old machine (not from trading). The partners work full time and a fair salary would be ₹30,000; none was charged. Normal profit for valuing goodwill = 1,20,000 + 15,000 − 10,000 − 30,000 = ₹95,000.

Questions students ask

What is super profit?

The excess of a firm's average profit over the normal profit, where normal profit is the normal rate of return applied to the capital employed.

Why is self-generated goodwill not shown in the books?

Because its cost cannot be measured reliably. Accounting Standard 26 allows only purchased goodwill, for which a price was paid, to be recorded.

What does "years' purchase" mean?

The number of years for which the buyer expects to enjoy the past level of profit or super profit. Goodwill is that profit multiplied by this number.
