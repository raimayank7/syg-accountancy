Bills of exchange: entries in the books of drawer and drawee

A bill of exchange turns a spoken promise to pay later into a signed paper that can be held, sold to a bank or passed on to someone else.

What a bill of exchange is

When goods are sold on credit, the seller may want something firmer than an entry in his books. He draws a bill ordering the buyer to pay a certain sum on a certain date, and the buyer signs his acceptance on it. Under the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, a bill of exchange is an instrument in writing containing an unconditional order, signed by the maker, directing a certain person to pay a certain sum of money only to, or to the order of, a certain person or to the bearer.

Parties

  • Drawer: the seller or creditor who makes the bill.
  • Drawee (acceptor): the buyer or debtor on whom it is drawn and who accepts it.
  • Payee: the person to whom payment is to be made. Often the drawer himself, or his bank or creditor.

A promissory note is different: it is a written promise made by the debtor himself, so it has two parties (maker and payee) and needs no acceptance.

Terms

  • Term (tenor): the period of the bill.
  • Days of grace: three extra days added to the term.
  • Due date (date of maturity): date of the bill + term + three days of grace. A bill dated 1 January for three months falls due on 4 April. If the due date is a public holiday, the bill is due on the preceding business day.
  • Bill at sight (on demand): payable when presented; no days of grace.
  • Bills receivable (B/R): the bill in the hands of the person who will receive money: an asset. Bills payable (B/P): the same bill for the person who must pay: a liability.

Dishonour of a bill

If Bina does not pay on the due date, the bill is dishonoured. The holder may have the fact recorded by a Notary Public; the fee is called noting charges and is recoverable from the acceptor. Suppose noting charges are ₹250. In every case Amit's entry debits Bina with ₹50,250, because she now owes him the bill and the charges. The credit depends on who held the bill.

Renewal of a bill

Bina may ask, before the due date, for more time. The old bill is cancelled and a new one is drawn, usually after part payment and with interest for the extra period.

Example. On 4 April Bina pays ₹20,000 in cash and accepts a new bill for two months for the balance with interest at 12% a year, and the noting charges. Balance = ₹50,250 − ₹20,000 = ₹30,250. Interest = ₹30,250 × 12% × 2/12 = ₹605. New bill = ₹30,855.

Retiring a bill

If the acceptor pays before the due date, the bill is retired. The holder usually allows a rebate for the unexpired period. For the holder the rebate is an expense; for the acceptor it is a gain.

Advantages of a bill

  • It is written proof of the debt and fixes the date of payment.
  • The seller can turn it into cash at once by discounting.
  • It can be passed on by endorsement to settle the seller's own debts.
  • The buyer gets a definite period of credit.

Questions students ask

How is the due date of a bill calculated?

Add the term of the bill to its date and then add three days of grace. A bill dated 1 January for three months is due on 4 April. A bill payable at sight has no days of grace.

Who bears noting charges?

The acceptor (drawee), because the charges arise from his failure to pay. The holder pays them first and recovers them from him.

What is the difference between a bill of exchange and a promissory note?

A bill is an order to pay, drawn by the creditor, with three parties, and needs acceptance. A promissory note is a promise to pay, made by the debtor, with two parties, and needs no acceptance.
